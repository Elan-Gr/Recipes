Pork Butt Smoking Planner

Pulled pork without the guesswork. Pork butt is the most forgiving cut on the smoker — a big collagen-rich shoulder that wants a long, slow cook to probe-tender. This planner covers the stall, the wrap decision, and the blade-bone test, then hands you to the main calculator to plan the cook to the minute. Pork butt at a glance

  • Pit temps: 225°F / 250°F / 275°F
  • Time rule of thumb: ~1.75 hr/lb at 225°F · ~1.5 hr/lb at 250°F · ~1.25 hr/lb at 275°F
  • Pull temp: 203°F (200 to 205°F range)
  • Rest: 45 min
  • Best woods: Hickory, apple, cherry, pecan

The blade-bone test

The thermometer says 203°F; the bone confirms it. On a bone-in butt, grab the blade bone and give it a twist — when the pork is done, the bone pulls clean with almost no resistance. If it holds on, the meat is not ready yet, whatever the thermometer says. Boneless butts cook a little faster and slice easier, but you lose the test and the bone's flavor.

The stall: wrap, or push through?

Somewhere between 150–165°F internal, the butt will park for hours — evaporative cooling, not a broken thermometer. Push through it for maximum bark, or wrap when the bark is set: foil braises and goes faster, butcher paper breathes and keeps bark better. Either way, the cook ends when it probes like warm butter, around 203°F.

Notes

  • Done is when it probes like warm butter and the blade bone twists free — usually around 203°F. Tenderness decides, not the number.
  • Rest at least 45 minutes before pulling; wrapped in towels in a dry cooler it holds for hours and gets better.
  • Got trimmed fat? Don't toss it — render it into lard, a clean cooking fat for frying and baking.
  • Fat side up or down: whichever faces your heat source. Protect the meat, not tradition.
