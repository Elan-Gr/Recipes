Turkey Smoking Planner

For holiday birds, planned to the minute. A turkey is the one cut where the pull temperature and the safety minimum are the same number — 165°F — so there is no margin for error. Turkey at a glance

  • Pit temps: 225°F / 250°F / 275°F (or 325°F hot and fast)
  • Time rule of thumb: whole bird ~0.65 hr/lb at 225°F · ~0.55 hr/lb at 250°F · ~0.45 hr/lb at 275°F
  • Pull temp: 165°F — and the USDA minimum is 165°F too
  • Rest: 20 min
  • Best woods: Cherry, apple, pecan, light hickory

Brine or not?

Brine at least 4 hours if you brine at all — a wet brine or a dry brine both work. Skip the brine if the bird is "enhanced," basted, or kosher: those are already salted, and more salt just makes it salty. Pat the skin dry before it goes on the pit; wet skin steams instead of crisping.

Whole vs spatchcocked

Spatchcocking — removing the backbone and flattening the bird — is the single biggest time saver in turkey smoking. The bird cooks evenly, the skin crisps, and the cook runs roughly an hour shorter at the same weight. Whole birds look better on the table; spatchcocked birds eat better.

Hot and fast at 325°F runs roughly 2–4 hours for a 10–20 lb whole bird. Poultry skin needs heat to render its fat and dry out — 225°F does not supply it, and the result is safe, cooked, and unpleasantly rubbery. Run birds at 275°F or higher, or finish them hot for the last twenty minutes.

Day-before checklist

  • Thaw: 24 hours per 4–5 lb in the fridge. A 15 lb bird needs 3–4 days.
  • Brine: start at least 4 hours before the cook (or skip it — see above).
  • Fuel and wood: enough charcoal or pellets for the full cook, plus cherry, apple, pecan, or light hickory.
  • Thermometer: a leave-in probe in the thickest part of the thigh, not touching bone.
  • Plan: work the cook backward from serving time with the main calculator, and add a buffer — a turkey that finishes early holds fine, a late one does not.

Notes

  • Pull at 165°F in the thickest part of the thigh. The breast will read a few degrees lower — that is normal.
  • Rest 20 minutes before carving so the juices settle.
  • Keep hot-held turkey above 140°F. Below that, the clock starts — 2 to 4 hours in the danger zone before food must be served or discarded.
  • A turkey that finishes early is a solved problem: wrap it and hold it. A late turkey ruins the meal — plan against the slow end and rest longer.
