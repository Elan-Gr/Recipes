Butter To Oil Conversion

Source: The Southern Lady Cooks (Judy Yeager, May 17, 2023) — <https://thesouthernladycooks.com/butter-to-oil-conversion-chart/>

Handy to keep on hand when you need to swap butter and oil in a recipe.

Can I replace butter with oil?

Yes — the ratio is 1:1. If the recipe calls for 1 cup of oil, use 1 cup of butter. This works with most oils (olive, vegetable, canola, coconut, etc.). If a recipe calls for 3/4 cup canola oil, melt 3/4 cup of butter instead.

Can I replace oil with butter?

Yes, in some cases, but the ratio is a little different. The original article includes a Butter to Oil Conversion Chart (as a downloadable PDF) with recommended amounts for the swap; the chart values were not captured in this text conversion — check the source link above.

When NOT to swap

Not all recipes will work when switching the two. Reasons:

  • Cakes that call for butter normally work better if you leave the butter in, especially a cake where you cream butter with sugar. The butter creates structure. That's why brownies and cake mixes work okay with oil — you're not creaming ingredients together. If the butter is just an added ingredient, you can probably switch it with oil.
  • You can always do a 50/50 split instead of replacing it all: use 50% butter and 50% oil.

Cooking with oil and butter

In a skillet the two are pretty interchangeable — if you're sautéing in oil, you can switch to butter. For frying, use oil for best results (a high-heat olive oil works well for frying).
