    Notes

    • The stall: somewhere between 145–165°F internal, the brisket will park for hours — that is evaporative cooling, not a broken thermometer. Wrap when the bark is set (dry-looking, doesn't smear when touched). Foil braises and goes faster; butcher/peach paper breathes and keeps bark better.
    • Done is when it probes like warm butter — usually 190°F sliced, 200°F+ pulled. Tenderness decides, not the number.
    • Rest at least an hour; wrapped in towels in a dry cooler it holds 4+ hours and gets better.
    • Fat side up or down: whichever faces your heat source. Protect the meat, not tradition.
    • Counter rest before the cook takes about 15 min off the cook time for a 45-minute rest — a warmer start cooks faster. Shorter rests take off proportionally less.
    • A whole packer loses 10–20% trimming the hard fat cap, deckle, and silverskin, then renders off another 30–40% of the trimmed weight during the smoke — roughly 50–55% total yield from raw to sliced. Plan on about 1/2 lb of cooked brisket per person, which is close to 1 lb of raw packer once trim and cook loss are figured in.
    • Got trimmed fat? Don't toss it — render it into beef tallow.
    • Wrap timing in the planner is an estimate (about two-thirds through the cook, around 165°F); the original calculator schedules the wrap at whatever point you choose. Watch the bark, not the clock.
