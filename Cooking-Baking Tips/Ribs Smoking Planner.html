Ribs at a glance

  • Pit temp: 225°F slow and low (300°F hot and fast)
  • Time rule of thumb: Baby backs fall-off-the-bone ~5 hr · 3-2-1 method ~6 hr · Spare ribs / St. Louis ~6 hr
  • Done: probe-tender, 195–205°F — but the bend test beats any thermometer
  • Rest: 10–15 min
  • Best woods: Hickory, apple, cherry

The 3-2-1 method is three hours of smoke, two hours wrapped, one hour sauced, all at 225°F. For the wrapped stage, add a little liquid — apple juice, butter, honey — and seal tight. For the finish, unwrap, sauce, and let the sauce set on the pit.

The bend test

There is no reliable temperature for ribs. Lift the rack with tongs from one end: done is when it bends and the surface cracks. Probe-tender runs about 195–205°F, but the bend is the test that matters. Overcooking turns baby backs to mush — leaner and smaller than spares, they finish roughly an hour sooner.

Sauce and spritz timing

Sauce in the last 20–30 minutes only — any earlier and the sugars burn before the meat is done. Spritz lightly once the bark is set, and don't soak the meat; every open lid costs heat and time.

Notes

  • Ribs are timed per rack, not per pound. Three racks in a rib rack take the same time as one — you are limited by grate area, not hours.
  • Rest 10–15 minutes before slicing so the juices settle.
  • Spare ribs are bigger and meatier than baby backs; plan about an hour longer.
