Raw Fish Rules

Source: Minnesota Department of Health — Safety of raw, raw-marinated, partially cooked, or marinated-partially cooked fish (January 2019). Contact: Minnesota Department of Health Food, Pools, and Lodging Services, PO Box 64975, St. Paul, MN 55164-0975; 651-201-4500; health.foodlodging@state.mn.us; www.health.state.mn.us

Freezing

Freezing to specific temperatures and times kills parasites in raw fish. You may obtain fish already frozen, or freeze fish yourself.

One of the following:

  • Freeze the fish and store at or below -4°F for at least 168 hours (7 days).
  • Freeze the fish at -31°F or below until solid AND store at -31°F or below for at least 15 hours.
  • Freeze the fish at -31°F or below until solid AND store at or below -4°F for at least 24 hours.

Record-keeping

If you serve or sell fish raw, raw-marinated, partially cooked, or marinated-partially cooked, you must keep records documenting the product is free of parasites. It is the duty of the PIC to obtain required documentation (e.g. a letter from your supplier) or maintain time/temperature records on site. Retain all records for 90 days after the fish is served or sold.

Supplier letters

If you obtain fish frozen to destroy parasites, request a letter from your supplier including:

  • Species of fish purchased
  • Statement of guarantee listing the temperature and time the fish has been frozen to meet required parameters

Time/temperature records

If you freeze fish at your establishment, record the species, freezing temperature and time, and dates. Contact your inspector for help developing procedures that comply with parasite destruction requirements.

All letters should include name, address, and contact information for both the supplier and your establishment.

Aquaculture

Freezing requirements apply to aquacultured fish that are fed live feed. There is reduced parasite hazard in aquacultured fish raised under specific conditions.

You may serve raw aquacultured fish without freezing if all of the following are true:

  • Fish were raised in net-pens in open waters OR in land-based operations such as ponds and tanks, AND
  • Fish were fed formulated feed that did not contain live parasites.

If you obtain aquacultured fish, request a letter from your supplier including:

  • Species of fish purchased
  • Statement of guarantee for net-pens or land-based operations
  • Statement of guarantee for parasite-free pellet feed

Exemptions

Some fish species or products have no specific parasite hazard and are exempt from freezing/aquaculture requirements:

  • Tuna of the species Thunnus alalunga, T. albacares, T. atlanticus, T. maccoyii, T. obesus, or T. thynnus (common names: Yellowfin, Bigeye, and Northern and Southern Bluefin)
  • Fish eggs removed from the skein and rinsed
  • Molluscan shellfish and shucked scallops
