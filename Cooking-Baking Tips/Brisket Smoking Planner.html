Brisket at a glance

  • Pit temps: Slow and Low 225°F / 107°C · Hot and Fast 300°F / 149°C
  • Time rule of thumb: Sliced (Slow and Low): ~75 min/lb · Sliced (Hot and Fast): ~35 min/lb · Pulled / Chopped (Slow and Low): ~82 min/lb
  • Rest: 3 hr recommended
  • Best woods: Oak, Hickory, Pecan, Mesquite

Sliced, pulled, or burnt ends?

Sliced is competition style — firm slices with a clean bite, pulled at ~190°F. Pulled/chopped goes further (~200°F) for sandwiches. Burnt ends take the point to pulled territory, then cube, sauce, and go back in the pit to candy up.

How long to smoke a brisket per pound

Slow and low at 225°F: about 75 minutes per pound for sliced (up to 13 lb; big packers run ~71 min/lb above that) and ~82 minutes per pound for pulled. Hot & fast at 300°F roughly halves it (~35–40 min/lb). The probe outranks the clock: sliced pulls around 190°F, pulled around 200°F, and either should slide like butter.

The stall, and when to wrap

Somewhere between 145–165°F internal, your brisket will park for hours. That's evaporative cooling, not a broken thermometer. On a big cook the stall can run hours 4–9 of the cook. Push through it for maximum bark, or wrap — foil braises and goes faster, butcher/peach paper breathes and keeps bark better. Wrap when the bark is set: dry-looking, doesn't smear when touched. The stall chart marks 165°F as the wrap point if you are wrapping, and 203°F as the point where the probe slides like butter.

A whole packer loses 10–20% up front trimming the hard fat cap, deckle, and silverskin, then renders off another 30–40% of that trimmed weight in fat and moisture during the smoke — roughly 50–55% total yield from raw to sliced. Plan on about 1/2 lb of cooked brisket per person, which is close to 1 lb of raw packer once trim and cook loss are figured in. Brisket goes fast and there's no margin if people go back for seconds, so this errs on a little extra — drop to Light if you're serving heavy sides or other meats.

Which form is the better deal?

Same cook, different buys — cheaper per pound usually means more knife work. A whole packer loses 10–20% to trimming but costs less per pound; a trimmed flat costs more per pound but you skip the trimming and the deckle. If you buy the packer, don't toss the trim — render it into beef tallow, gold for cooking, seasoning cast iron, candles, even skincare.

Notes

  • Done is when it probes like warm butter — usually 190°F sliced, 200°F+ pulled. Tenderness decides, not the number.
  • Rest at least an hour; wrapped in towels in a dry cooler it holds 4+ hours and gets better.
  • Fat side up or down: whichever faces your heat source. Protect the meat, not tradition.
  • Counter rest before the cook takes about 15 min off the cook time for a 45-minute rest — a warmer start cooks faster. Shorter rests take off proportionally less.
  • Got trimmed fat? Don't toss it — render it into beef tallow.
