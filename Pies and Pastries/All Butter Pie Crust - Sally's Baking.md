# All Butter Pie Crust

Source: Sally's Baking Addiction — <https://sallysbakingaddiction.com/all-butter-pie-crust/>

4.7 from 159 reviews. Buttery, flaky, and tender, with incredible flavor.

**Author:** Sally McKenney · **Prep:** 20 minutes · **Cook:** 0 minutes · **Total:** 2 hours, 20 minutes · **Yield:** 2 pie crusts

## Ingredients

- 2 and 1/2 cups (313g) **all-purpose flour**, plus more as needed (spooned & leveled)
- 2 teaspoons **granulated sugar**
- 1 teaspoon **salt**
- 1 cup (16 Tbsp; 226g) **unsalted butter**, chilled and cubed
- 1/2 cup (120ml) **ice water**, plus more as needed

## Instructions

1. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, and salt. Add the cold cubed butter on top. Using a pastry cutter, food processor, or two forks (pastry cutter is ideal), cut or pulse the butter into the dry ingredients until it resembles coarse meal. You're looking for pea-sized bits of flour-coated butter. A few larger bits of butter is OK.
2. Drizzle the cold water over the surface of the flour mixture, 1 tablespoon at a time, stirring after each addition. Stop adding water when the dough comes together easily and begins to form large clumps. The dough will feel moist and a little sticky, but not overly wet. Do not add any more water than you need. (About 1/2 cup / 120ml ice water is typical.)
3. Transfer the dough to a lightly floured work surface. Using floured hands, gently bring the dough together into a ball, avoiding overworking it. If it feels too dry or crumbly, dip your fingers in cold water and continue. If it feels too sticky, sprinkle on a little more flour. Using a sharp knife or bench scraper, divide the dough in half. Gently flatten each half into a 1-inch-thick disc.
4. Wrap each disc tightly in plastic wrap and refrigerate for at least 2 hours and up to 5 days before using.
5. After refrigerating, the dough is ready to roll out and use in a pie recipe. **Tip:** When rolling out the chilled discs, use gentle force. Start from the center and work out in all directions, turning the dough between rolls. Smooth out edges if you notice cracks. Keep the work surface, rolling pin, and hands lightly floured.

## Notes

- **Make ahead:** Prepare through step 4 and freeze the discs for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before using.
- **Pie crust comparison:** Due to butter's high water content, this all-butter crust is lighter textured with more defined flakes — the butter's water converts to steam in the oven, creating thousands of light flakes. A shortening-and-butter crust is a little easier to work with (shortening has a higher melting point, so it holds its shape better). For intricate pie crust designs, the shortening-and-butter version is recommended.
- **Try this:** Replacing 1 tablespoon of ice water with cold apple cider vinegar creates an even flakier, more tender pie dough.
