Rhubarb Pie

Source: Well Plated by Erin Clarke — <https://www.wellplated.com/rhubarb-pie/>

5 from 4 reviews. Classic rhubarb pie bakes sweet-tart rhubarb into a jammy filling under a flaky crust — a bright spring dessert.

Prep: 30 mins · Cook: 1 hr 15 mins · Total: 5 hrs 45 mins · Servings: 8

Ingredients

  • 1 double crust pie crust, your favorite (or store-bought, enough for two 10-inch rounds)
  • 5 cups sliced rhubarb, about 1 1/3 pounds; if frozen, do not thaw
  • ¾ cup plus 1 teaspoon granulated sugar, divided
  • 5 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • ⅛ teaspoon kosher salt
  • ⅛ teaspoon freshly grated nutmeg (optional)
  • 1 tablespoon unsalted butter, cut into small pieces
  • 1 large egg, beaten with 1 teaspoon water, for egg wash
  • 1 tablespoon turbinado or similar coarse sugar
  • Whipped cream (optional, for serving)

Instructions

  1. Prepare the pie crust and chill for 1 hour as directed (or up to 2 days). When ready to bake, place a rack in the center of the oven and preheat to 425°F.
  2. Divide the dough in two, making one half slightly larger than the other. Return the smaller half to the refrigerator.
  3. On a moderately floured work surface (a piece of parchment or wax paper makes the dough easy to move), roll out the larger half into a 12-inch circle (if too stiff from the fridge, let it rest at room temperature a few minutes until rollable). Work from the center of the dough outward, rotating the dough as you go — this gives a better, more even circle with less tearing, and lets you check whether the dough is sticking. Work fast, because the dough gets sticky as it warms. Flour the work surface and rolling pin as little as is manageable. If the dough gets too sticky to handle at any point, pop it back in the fridge for a few minutes. If there are any tears or cracks, just patch them together.
  4. Transfer the dough to a regular 9-inch pie plate (not deep dish) — fold the circle over the rolling pin, then use the rolling pin to unfurl it gently into the plate. Ease the dough down into the plate (don't pull to stretch it or it will bounce back). Pop into the freezer.
  5. On a sheet of parchment or wax paper, roll out the second (smaller) portion of dough into an 11-inch circle; for a lattice, cut it into 1-inch strips. Use the paper to place it in the refrigerator while you prepare the filling.
  6. In a large bowl, stir together the rhubarb, 3/4 cup granulated sugar, flour, salt, and nutmeg.
  7. Remove the bottom crust from the freezer. Sprinkle the remaining 1 teaspoon granulated sugar over the bottom. With a large spoon, transfer all of the rhubarb mixture (including any juices if the rhubarb is fresh, or dry bits of flour and sugar if frozen) to the plate, spreading into an even layer.
  8. Scatter the butter over the top of the rhubarb filling.
  9. Drape the top crust over the filling (or arrange the lattice). Trim to 1/2-inch overhang all the way around. Tuck the top crust under the bottom crust. Crimp the edges with your fingers (or press along the edges with the tines of a fork). If not using a lattice, cut 5 to 6 vents in the top with a sharp knife.
  10. Use a dry brush to brush away excess flour from the crust, then brush the crust all over with the egg wash.
  11. Place the pie on a parchment-lined baking sheet and sprinkle with the turbinado sugar. Bake on the center rack at 425°F for 15 minutes, then reduce the temperature to 375°F. Continue baking until the crust is deep golden and you see bits of filling bubbling out of the vents, about 50 to 55 minutes more, rotating the pan 180° halfway through (if the rhubarb was frozen, extend the baking time by 10 minutes or more). If the crust edges get too dark at any point, shield with foil or a pie crust guard.
  12. Let the pie cool at room temperature completely — at least 4 hours. Slice and enjoy with whipped cream, or nothing at all.

Notes

  • Sugar: As written, the amount of sugar yields a tart, rhubarb-forward pie. If you like your pie sweeter, add an additional 1/4 cup.
  • Egg wash substitute: If you'd rather not use an egg, brush the crust with half-and-half, cream, or milk.
  • Serving: Rhubarb tastes better without ice cream; if you'd like a topping, serve with whipped cream.
  • To store: Wrap cooled pie in foil or plastic wrap, or transfer leftovers to an airtight container. Store at room temperature up to 5 days.
  • To freeze: Freeze up to 2 months; thaw in the refrigerator before serving.
