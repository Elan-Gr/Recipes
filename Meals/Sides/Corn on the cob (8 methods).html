Corn on the cob (8 methods)

By Jesse Szewczyk (The Kitchn) · Updated Jun 6, 2025

Quick Overview

Winner: Grilling with the husks on. The husks steam the corn, keeping it moist and preventing it from drying out. The charred husks give bold, smoky flavor without drying or burned bits.

Runner-up: Boiling corn in salted water. No charred flavor, but kernels come out plump, juicy, and perfectly salted.

Notes on Methodology

  • The methods: Searched the internet for the most reliable and trusted methods of cooking corn on the cob; landed on the eight popular methods below.
  • The testing: All corn cooked on the same day, cobs bought at the same time.
  • The ratings: Judged on ease of preparation and flavor — plump, golden, crunchy-yet-tender kernels full of flavor, without spending a ton of time.

Method: Microwave

  • Total time: 5 minutes · Rating: 1/10
  • About: Wrap whole, shucked cobs of corn in damp paper towels, place on a plate, and microwave for 5 minutes.
  • Results: Corn turned out dry and shriveled; kernels dried out, shrunk, turned popcorn-like. Disappointing — not even a generous smearing of butter saved it.
  • Takeaway: Quickest and easiest, but not worth the time saved; dries out and becomes practically inedible.

Method: Grilling Without the Husks

  • Total time: 20 minutes · Rating: 2/10
  • About: Preheat grill for 10 minutes, throw shucked corn directly on the grates, grill for 10 minutes, rotating often so it doesn't burn.
  • Results: Shriveled, burnt, way-too-dry kernels; lost all freshness and moisture.
  • Takeaway: Corn can't withstand direct grill heat without a barrier (foil or husks). Not recommended.

Method: Instant Pot

  • Total time: 15 minutes (including pressurization) · Rating: 7/10
  • About: Shuck and halve corn, line up vertically on the trivet insert. Cook on HIGH pressure for 5 minutes, quick release. Melt butter in the still-warm insert and pour over the cobs.
  • Results: Similar to boiled corn — plump, juicy, bright yellow kernels, tender without being overcooked. Melting butter in the insert avoids dirtying another dish.
  • Takeaway: Great if pinched for time and you own an Instant Pot. Delicious, simple, tender, moist; unfussy cleanup.

Method: Wrapped in Foil and Grilled

  • Total time: 15 minutes · Rating: 7/10
  • About: Shuck corn cobs, wrap each in foil with a little butter inside. Cook directly on the grill for 15 minutes, then unwrap and serve.
  • Results: Perfectly cooked, snappy, tender, bright yellow, moist — but bland; didn't taste grilled, just like steamed corn with butter.
  • Takeaway: Tender and juicy but lacks smoky flavor; no better than boiled corn despite more effort.

Method: Cooking in a Butter Bath

  • Total time: 15 minutes · Rating: 7.5/10
  • About: Bring a large pot of milk or water to a boil, add a generous amount of butter, and simmer shucked corn cobs until tender (about 8 minutes).
  • Results: Perfectly plump, coated in butter, tender without being mushy. Didn't have to stir or keep them submerged. The milk likely added nothing; add a generous pinch of salt, or use salted butter.
  • Takeaway: Butter bath coats cobs in a thin layer of butter — glossy, buttery, rich. Probably fine to just throw a stick of butter in boiling salted water.

Method: Roasting in the Oven

  • Total time: 35 minutes · Rating: 7.7/10
  • About: Line up whole corn cobs (husks still attached) directly on oven grates and roast at 350°F for 35 minutes.
  • Results: Husks burned and blackened, smelling like a summer festival; the corn itself was moist, tender, juicy, with a deep smoky flavor; didn't burn or shrivel; stayed bright yellow.
  • Takeaway: Great way to mimic grilling without a grill. The charred husks lend smoky flavor while protecting the corn. Drawback: the corn itself didn't get any color, so slightly less flavorful than grilled.

Method: Boiling in Salted Water

  • Total time: 25 minutes · Rating: 8/10
  • About: Place whole, shucked corn cobs in boiling, salted water and boil for 5 minutes. Can keep in the water an additional 10 minutes to keep warm; longer than that will result in tough corn.
  • Results: Perfectly cooked, perfectly seasoned. The salt made the corn intensely flavorful without tasting salty. Plump, juicy, bright yellow kernels.
  • Takeaway: Easy, perfectly seasoned corn with no special tools or effort. Sometimes the simplest methods are best.

Method: Grilling with the Husks On

  • Total time: 20 minutes · Rating: 10/10
  • About: Trim the silks off the corn, then, with husks still attached, throw them onto a grill set to high heat. Cook, rotating often, for about 15 minutes, then serve.
  • Results: Absolutely incredible. Husks turned completely black but the corn was still perfectly plump, bright yellow, and moist. Charred husks gave a deep smoky flavor — savory and bold, like fairground corn.
  • Takeaway: The perfect balance of flavor and texture. The husks protect the corn from drying out and burning while lending deep smoky flavor. Hands-down the best method: sweet, smoky, and savory.
