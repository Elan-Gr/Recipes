Easy Chinese Egg Pancake (Ji Dan Bing)

From Shuangy's Kitchen Sink — tender, chewy, savory egg crepes (鸡蛋饼), a nostalgic Chinese breakfast. Thin like a crepe, savory like an omelet, ready in 20 minutes. Eat plain, roll up, or use as a wrap.

Ingredients

  • 3 large eggs (~150 g)
  • 200 g all-purpose flour (about 1⅔ cups)
  • 200 ml water (about ¾ cup + 1 tablespoon)
  • 1 stalk scallion (~15 g), roots removed and finely chopped
  • ½ teaspoon (~3 g) salt, or to taste
  • Pinch of white pepper, or to taste (optional)
  • Sugar, to taste (optional)
  • Butter or oil spray, for greasing the pan

Yield: 8 pancakes

Instructions

  1. In a large mixing bowl, whisk together the eggs, flour, and water until smooth. The batter should be thin.
  2. Season with salt (and white pepper if using), then fold in the chopped scallion.
  3. Heat a 10.5–11 inch nonstick pan over medium-high heat and lightly grease with butter or oil spray.
  4. Add ¼ to ⅓ cup of batter and immediately tilt and swirl the pan so it spreads into a thin, even layer.
  5. Cook until the center is set (about 1 minute), then flip and cook the other side until lightly golden — 2–3 minutes total per pancake.
  6. Repeat with the remaining batter, greasing the pan as needed. Serve warm.

Tips

  • Move fast: the thin batter cooks quickly, so swirl the pan the moment the batter hits it.
  • Thinner batter makes softer, more tender pancakes — add a splash of water if it feels too thick to swirl.
  • Measurements assume a 10.5–11 inch pan; for a bigger pan, increase the batter a little to keep the same thickness.
  • More flour makes them chewier; less makes them thinner and softer — adjust to taste.
  • Sweet version: whisk sugar to taste into the batter along with the salt.
