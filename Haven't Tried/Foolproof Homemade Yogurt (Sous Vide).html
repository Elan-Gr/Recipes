Foolproof Homemade Yogurt (Sous Vide)

Making your own yogurt is dead simple. Start with yogurt with live active cultures (check the label). Save at least ¼ cup of each batch for the next one. Sous vide avoids the vigilant pot-watching of the stovetop method — no risk of scorched milk or rubbery skin.

Source: Sous Vide at Home, p. 260 (photo of cookbook page)

Makes: about 1 quart · Sous vide time: 5 to 10 hours, to taste · Active prep: 5 minutes, plus 3½ hours to cool

Ingredients

  • 4 cups whole milk
  • 2 tablespoons nonfat milk powder (optional)
  • ¼ cup whole-milk plain yogurt with live active cultures, store-bought or from a previous batch

Method

  1. Preheat the sous vide water bath to 85°C (185°F).
  2. Combine the milk and milk powder (if using) in a gallon-size freezer-safe ziplock bag; seal using the water displacement or table-edge method.
  3. When the water reaches temperature, lower the bagged milk into the bath (fully submerged) and cook for 1 hour.
  4. Remove the bag and set aside until warm but not hot to the touch, about 30 minutes (110°F–120°F on a thermometer is ideal; cool enough to touch is fine). While the milk cools, lower the bath to 46°C (114.8°F).
  5. Once cooled, add the yogurt, mix well to combine thoroughly, and reseal the bag (whisk it in or massage the sealed bag to distribute evenly).
  6. Lower the bag back into the bath and culture for 4 hours or up to 9 hours, to taste. Done at the 4-hour mark (thickens somewhat as it cools); longer gives thicker, tangier yogurt.
  7. Remove from the bath and refrigerate at least 3 hours until cold. Transfer to mason jars or an airtight container if desired.

Do-ahead

Keeps in the refrigerator up to 2 weeks. Save ¼ cup to start the next batch.
