Cinnamon Streusel Coffee Cake

Source: King Arthur Baking — <https://www.kingarthurbaking.com/recipes/cinnamon-streusel-coffee-cake-recipe>

Soft and moist coffee cake with plenty of buttery crumbs and a generous stripe of rich brown sugar filling. For a coffee cake with less filling, where the cake itself is the main attraction, reduce the brown sugar in the filling to 1/3 cup (71g), keeping the remaining ingredients the same.

Prep: 30 mins · Bake: 50 mins to 1 hr · Total: 1 hr 30 mins · Yield: one 9" x 13" or two 9" round coffee cakes

Ingredients

Topping

  • 1 cup (198g) granulated sugar
  • 1/4 teaspoon table salt (if you use unsalted butter)
  • 1 cup (120g) King Arthur Unbleached All-Purpose Flour
  • 1 tablespoon ground cinnamon
  • 6 tablespoons (85g) butter, melted

Filling

  • 1 cup (213g) dark brown sugar or light brown sugar, packed
  • 1 1/2 tablespoons ground cinnamon
  • 1 teaspoon unsweetened cocoa, Dutch-process or natural (used strictly for color, not flavor; leave it out if you like)

Cake

  • 12 tablespoons (170g) butter, at room temperature (at least 65°F)
  • 1 teaspoon table salt (1 1/4 teaspoons if you use unsalted butter)
  • 1 1/2 cups (298g) granulated sugar
  • 1/3 cup (71g) light brown sugar or dark brown sugar, packed
  • 2 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
  • 2 teaspoons King Arthur Pure Vanilla Extract
  • 3 large eggs, at room temperature
  • 3/4 cup (170g) sour cream or plain yogurt, at room temperature
  • 1 1/4 cups (283g) milk (anything from skim to whole), at room temperature
  • 3 3/4 cups (450g) King Arthur Unbleached All-Purpose Flour

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F. Lightly grease a 9" x 13" pan, or two 9" round pans.
  2. To make the topping: In a medium bowl, whisk together the sugar, salt, flour, and cinnamon. Add the melted butter, stirring until well combined. Set aside.
  3. To make the filling: In a small bowl, mix together the brown sugar, cinnamon, and cocoa powder. Set aside.
  4. To make the batter: In a large bowl or the bowl of a stand mixer, beat together the butter, salt, sugars, baking powder, and vanilla until well combined and smooth. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition.
  5. In a separate bowl or liquid measuring cup, whisk together the sour cream or yogurt and milk until well combined; some lumps are OK.
  6. Add the flour to the butter mixture alternately with the milk/sour cream mixture, beating gently to combine.
  7. Pour/spread half the batter (a scant 3 cups or about 790g) into the prepared pan(s), spreading all the way to the edges. (If using two 9" round pans, spread 1 1/3 cups / about 395g batter in each pan.) Sprinkle the filling evenly over the batter.
  8. Spread the remaining batter atop the filling. Use a table knife or small offset spatula to gently swirl the filling into the batter, as though making a marble cake. Don't combine filling and batter thoroughly; just swirl the filling through the batter.
  9. Sprinkle the topping over the batter in the pan.
  10. Bake until dark golden brown around the edges, medium-golden with no light patches on top, and a toothpick or thin knife inserted into the center comes out clean — about 55 to 60 minutes for the 9" x 13" pan, 50 to 55 minutes for the 9" round pans. When pressed gently in the middle, the coffee cake should spring back.
  11. Remove from the oven and cool for 20 minutes before cutting and serving. Serve right from the pan.

Storage

Store leftover coffee cake, well wrapped, at room temperature for several days; freeze for longer storage.

Tips from our Bakers

  • Glaze: Dress it up with a simple glaze: whisk together 1/2 cup (57g) confectioners' sugar and 1 tablespoon (14g) milk; drizzle over the cooled coffee cake.
  • Quality ingredients: Since butter and sour cream are both key ingredients, use the best quality you can get.
  • Make ahead: Cover the unbaked cake with plastic wrap and refrigerate overnight. Next morning, bake as directed, starting to test for doneness at the end of the suggested baking time — you'll probably need to add about 5 minutes to account for the chilled batter.
